### The Anatomy of the Symmetry‑Lighting Score  

#### How the Model Quantifies Symmetry  

Computer‑vision engines first map facial landmarks—eyes, nose, mouth, jawline—using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The distances between paired landmarks (e.g., left eye to right eye) are normalised against inter‑pupillary distance to produce a symmetry ratio. A deviation of less than 3 % typically lands a profile in the “high‑symmetry” bucket, which the algorithm then flags for preferential placement in the feed.

#### Lighting as a Proxy for Photographic Quality  

Lighting is parsed through a separate luminance‑contrast module. The model extracts histogram data, evaluates the ratio of shadow to highlight pixels, and checks for colour temperature consistency. Images that exhibit soft, diffused lighting (often achieved with ring lights or AI‑enhanced soft‑focus filters) receive a higher “clarity” multiplier, boosting the overall attractiveness score.

#### Weighting and Rank Integration  

In 2026, most mainstream apps apply a linear combination:  

`Final Score = (0.45 × Symmetry Ratio) + (0.35 × Lighting Clarity) + (0.20 × User‑Generated Interaction Metrics)`

The interaction metrics—reply speed, swipe reciprocity— are deliberately kept low weight to prevent the system from being hijacked solely by visual tricks. Nonetheless, a flawless visual score can catapult a profile into the top 5 % of the daily candidate pool, regardless of behavioural signals.

### Forensic Verification Protocols  

#### 1. Spectrogram Audio Analysis via Audacity  

Even the most polished photo cannot mask a voice that has been spliced from a public source. Record a brief voice note from the contact and open it in Audacity. Generate a spectrogram; look for repeated frequency patterns or abrupt transitions that suggest stitching. Genuine speech exhibits a smooth harmonic progression, whereas synthetic or edited audio shows blocky artefacts at regular intervals.

#### 2. Cross‑Engine Reverse Image Search  

Upload the profile picture to at least two independent reverse‑image services (e.g., Google Lens and TinEye). A match on a stock‑photo repository, a modelling portfolio, or a different social platform indicates reuse. Pay particular attention to variations in background, clothing, or cropping—these often betray AI‑generated composites that have been subtly altered to evade detection.

#### 3. Unscheduled 30‑Second Video Liveness Check  

Ask the prospect to initiate a spontaneous video call lasting no more than thirty seconds. During the call, request a simple, non‑repetitive motion—tilt the head, blink twice, or read a random phrase. Record the session locally and later analyse frame‑by‑frame for facial landmark drift. Deep‑fake avatars tend to exhibit jitter or lag when responding to unpredictable gestures.

#### 4. EXIF and Metadata Scrutiny  

Download the image and inspect its EXIF data with a tool such as ExifTool. Authentic smartphone captures usually contain a coherent set of tags: camera make/model, aperture, ISO, and a plausible timestamp. Missing or generic tags (e.g., “Adobe Photoshop”) suggest post‑processing, which may include AI‑driven symmetry enhancement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do all dating apps use the same symmetry‑lighting algorithm?**  
A: No. While the core principle—mapping facial landmarks and assessing illumination—is common, each platform calibrates its weighting and model architecture differently. Some may prioritise colour balance over symmetry, while niche apps might discard visual scoring altogether in favour of questionnaire‑based compatibility.

**Q: Can AI‑generated faces ever pass these forensic checks?**  
A: Advanced generative models can produce highly convincing stills, but they often leave subtle artefacts: inconsistent shadow direction, unnatural eye reflections, or a lack of micro‑expressions. The spectrogram and live‑video checks are particularly effective because they require temporal coherence that static generators cannot replicate.

**Q: Is it safe to share a voice note for verification?**  
A: Sharing a brief voice snippet carries minimal risk, provided you do not disclose personally identifiable information (e.g., full name, address). Use the Audacity spectrogram method locally; no third‑party service needs to process the audio.

**Q: How often should I perform these checks on new matches?**  
A: Apply the verification protocol the first time you encounter a profile that exhibits any of the telemetry anomalies listed in the hook—delayed typing, instant external redirects, or overly polished images. Re‑assessment is advisable if the interaction pattern changes dramatically (e.g., sudden increase in response speed).
