## Field Hook & Context  

In the last twelve months the average user on mainstream dating platforms registers a median of 2,800 swipe actions per month, with response latency often exceeding the natural conversational pause of 2–3 seconds. The telemetry tells a story:  

* **Delayed responses** – a 12‑second lag on a “hey” that should be a reflexive “hey” suggests a bot‑mediated queue or an exhausted interlocutor.  
* **Unnatural typing cadence** – uniform 0.45 second keystroke intervals betray copy‑and‑paste scripts, a hallmark of automated outreach.  
* **LLM token repetition** – identical phrasing across dozens of profiles points to large‑language‑model generated bios, eroding authentic connection.  
* **Immediate WhatsApp redirects** – a sudden jump from in‑app chat to external messaging within three messages is a classic pressure‑point for phishing.  
* **Suspicious image EXIF data** – timestamps clustered at 02:13 GMT across unrelated accounts reveal stock‑photo reuse.

These signals, when accumulated, generate a cognitive load that manifests as “dating‑app burnout”: a state of emotional exhaustion, decision fatigue, and diminished trust in digital courtship. The following protocol is a forensic‑style de‑escalation plan, designed for the modern dater who wishes to reset their emotional bandwidth without discarding the possibility of future connections.

### 1. Mapping the Anatomy of Burnout  

Burnout on dating apps is not merely “tiredness”; it is a measurable overload of three cognitive vectors:

1. **Information Saturation** – endless profiles, endless prompts, endless “what‑are‑you‑looking‑for” boxes.  
2. **Emotional Volatility** – the dopamine spikes from matches followed by the troughs of ghosting.  
3. **Trust Erosion** – repeated exposure to deceptive signals lowers the baseline for what is considered trustworthy.

A forensic analyst would log each vector, noting frequency and intensity. For the everyday user, a simple spreadsheet suffices: column A for date, B for number of matches, C for “positive interactions” (genuine conversation lasting >5 minutes), D for “red‑flag events” (e.g., immediate external link). When the ratio of red‑flags to positive interactions exceeds 0.3, the system flags a burnout risk.

### 2. Day‑Zero: Secure the Perimeter  

Before the 14‑day clock starts, lock down the digital environment:

- **Deactivate push notifications** on all dating apps.  
- **Export conversation logs** to a local encrypted folder for later forensic review.  
- **Run a reverse‑image search** on every profile picture you have kept. Tools such as Google Images or TinEye will reveal reuse across unrelated accounts.  
- **Audit audio messages** with Audacity: generate a spectrogram and look for background loops or synthetic speech signatures.  

These steps provide a baseline “clean‑room” snapshot, ensuring that any subsequent emotional shift can be attributed to the protocol rather than hidden variables.

### 4. Verifying Post‑Reset Interactions  

If you choose to re‑enter the digital dating sphere after day 14, each new interaction should undergo a lightweight forensic triage:

1. **Image Verification** – run a reverse‑search on the profile picture. A single hit on a stock‑photo site is a red flag.  
2. **Audio Spectrogram** – if a voice note is received, open it in Audacity, generate a spectrogram, and look for uniform frequency bands indicative of synthetic speech.  
3. **Live‑Video Liveness Check** – request a spontaneous 30‑second video call. Ask the interlocutor to perform a simple, unpredictable gesture (e.g., “touch your left ear”). Record the session locally for later review if needed.  

These checks are low‑tech but effective, and they reinforce a habit of evidence‑based trust building.

### How do I know if my burnout is severe enough to warrant a full reset?

### Can I shorten the 14‑day period if I feel better sooner?  
The protocol’s strength lies in its staged exposure. Skipping stages—particularly the full digital abstinence—often leads to a rebound of the same stressors. If you must adjust, keep at least 7 days of total abstinence before any re‑entry test.

### Is the risk calculator reliable without sending data to a server?

### What if I encounter a match who passes all forensic checks but still feels “off”?
