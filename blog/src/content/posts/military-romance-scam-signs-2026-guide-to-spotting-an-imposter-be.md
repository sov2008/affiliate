## 1️⃣ Verify the Uniform & ID  

### Action Protocol  
1. **Ask for a clear photo of a current military ID** (e.g., DD‑214, CAC, or service‑specific badge).  
2. **Cross‑check the name, rank, and branch** on an official database (e.g., [DoD’s Public Service Records](https://publicservice.dod.mil)).  
3. **Look for inconsistencies**—wrong uniform insignia, outdated ribbons, or mismatched service dates.  

### Chat Example  

| **You** | **Scammer** |
|---|---|
| *“Hey Alex, could you send a photo of your current ID? I’m just being extra cautious.”* | *“Sure, here’s a pic of my badge. It’s a bit blurry because I’m in a base tent.”* |
| *“Thanks! I noticed the insignia looks like an Army Air Force badge—are you sure you’re Army?”* | *“Oh, I didn’t realize! I’m actually in the Air Force, sorry for the mix‑up.”* |

**Red Flag:** The scammer either refuses, provides a stock photo, or offers an implausible explanation for the error.

## 2️⃣ Scrutinize the Story & Timing  

### Action Protocol  
- **Map the timeline:** Deployments, leaves, and “being overseas” should align with known military cycles.  
- **Check the geography:** Verify the base location via Google Earth or the branch’s base list.  
- **Question urgency:** Phrases like “I need to send money now before my pay is cut off” are classic pressure tactics.  

### Chat Example  

| **You** | **Scammer** |
|---|---|
| *“You mentioned you’re stationed in ‘Fort Freedom.’ I can’t find that base—where is it?”* | *“It’s a secret forward operating base in the Middle East. I can’t give the exact location.”* |
| *“I understand secrecy, but can you share a generic map of the region?”* | *“Sorry, I’m not allowed to share any maps.”* |

**Red Flag:** Vague or secretive location details, especially when the user can’t provide any verifiable evidence.

## 3️⃣ Analyze Communication Patterns  

### Action Protocol  
- **Language consistency:** Scammers often switch between formal military jargon and slang.  
- **Emoji overload:** Excessive hearts, flags, or “💣💥” emojis can indicate a scripted persona.  
- **Response latency:** Sudden “away” messages followed by rapid, overly affectionate replies are manipulation tactics.  

### Chat Example  

| **You** | **Scammer** |
|---|---|
| *“What’s a typical day for you on base?”* | *“It’s crazy busy 😅💣💥! Lots of drills, PT, and then I’m thinking about you 😘❤️.”* |
| *“Cool, what’s your MOS?”* | *“I’m a *—* (no answer) — just a soldier, love.”* |

**Red Flag:** Dodging concrete answers, over‑use of emojis, and shifting tone dramatically after a pause.

## 4️⃣ Protect Your Money & Personal Data  

### Action Protocol  
1. **Never share banking info** or send funds, even for “transport fees” or “emergency supplies.”  
2. **Set up a financial safety net:** Use a prepaid card for any legitimate military expenses you might need to cover.  
3. **Report suspicious activity** to the platform, the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), and the **U.S. Army’s Family Assistance Center**.  

### Chat Example  

| **You** | **Scammer** |
|---|---|
| *“I can’t send the $1,200 right now—my account is frozen. Can you cover it until I sort it out?”* | *“Sure, I’ll send you a PayPal link. It’s urgent—my family needs the meds.”* |
| *“I’m sorry, I can’t do that. Let’s keep talking and verify everything first.”* | *“Fine, you’re being difficult. I’ll find someone else then.”* |

**Red Flag:** Immediate financial requests, especially with emotional blackmail or “once‑only” urgency.
