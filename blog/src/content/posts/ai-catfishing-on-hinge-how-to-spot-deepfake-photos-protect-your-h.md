**Visual Forensics: Decoding Deepfake Photos**

Reverse-image search has become an essential tool in the fight against deepfakes. Here's how to use it:

### 1.1 Reverse-Image Search with a Twist

1. Copy the image URL (right-click → "Copy image address").
2. Paste into Google Lens, TinEye, and the new MetaVision AI (2026 release).
3. Look for multiple origins: identical images on unrelated sites, stock-photo repositories, or AI-art platforms (e.g., Midjourney, Stable Diffusion).

**Chat Example:**

| You | Hinge Match |
|-----|--------------|
| “Hey, love your travel pic! Where was that taken?” | “Oh, that’s a beach in Bali. I’m actually from New York.” |
| *You run a reverse-image search and find the same photo on a stock-photo site.* | *You reply:* “Cool! I found the same picture on a stock-photo site—was it a professional shoot?” |

If the match hesitates or gives a vague answer, flag the profile.

### 1.2 Metadata & EXIF Analysis

Use the ExifTool browser extension (2026 update) to read hidden data. Authentic smartphone photos usually contain GPS, device model, and timestamp. Deepfakes often have blank or generic metadata (e.g., “Image created with Photoshop”).

### 1.3 AI-Detection Overlays

Install the DeepDetect Chrome plugin (free in 2026). It overlays a confidence meter (0–100%) on every image. Anything above 70% warrants a deeper dive.

**Conversational Red Flags & Voice-AI Checks**

Consistency audits are essential in identifying potential AI-catfish. Here's how to conduct one:

### 2.1 Consistency Audits

Ask location-specific questions that require real-world knowledge (e.g., “What’s the best coffee shop on Main St. near the river?”). AI-catfish often generate generic answers or repeat phrasing.

**Chat Example:**

| You | Match |
|-----|-------|
| “I’m craving a latte. Any hidden gems near the 5th-Ave subway?” | “There’s a great spot on 5th-Ave. It’s popular.” |
| *You follow up:* “Which one? The one with the mural or the rooftop?” | *Match stalls or gives a vague “I don’t know, I haven’t been there.”* |

### 2.2 Voice Message Verification

If a match sends a voice note, run it through an open-source spectrogram analyzer (such as Audacity) or live unscripted questions. Look for synthetic speech artifacts: overly smooth intonation, lack of breaths, or a “flat” emotional range.

**Dialogue Prompt:**

> “Hey, could you send a quick voice note saying ‘I love hiking on Saturdays’? I’m testing my new voice-auth app.”  

A genuine reply will have natural pauses; a deepfake will sound too perfect.

**Financial Safeguards – Stop the Wallet Drain**

### 3.1 The “Emergency Help” Test

1. Ask for proof: “Can you share a photo of the bill or a screenshot of the hospital receipt?”

3. Delay & Report: If anything feels off, pause the conversation and report to Hinge and FlirtCheck.

Legitimate dating matches never solicit funds. If financial topics or payment requests emerge, immediately terminate contact and report the account to app moderators and the FTC / FBI IC3.
