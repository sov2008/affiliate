## 1. The Anatomy of a Modern Pig‑Butchering Scam  

### 1.1 How Fraudsters Build Trust  

| Stage | Tactics | Typical Dialogue |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **Lure** | Attractive photos, “travel blogger” vibe, shared hobbies | “Hey! I love hiking too – just got back from Patagonia 🌄” |
| **Bond** | Daily messages, emotional confessions, “I’ve never met anyone like you” | “You’re the first person who really gets me. I feel so safe talking to you.” |
| **Investment Pitch** | Introduce “crypto fund”, “high‑yield WhatsApp group”, or “Sha Zhu Pan” scheme | “I’m part of a private crypto pool that’s up 150 % this month. Want in? I can send the link.” |
| **Escalation** | Urgency (“limited seats”), fear (“market closing”), and pressure to move funds quickly | “We have only 2 spots left – you need to transfer before midnight or you’ll miss out.” |
| **Extraction** | Requests for wallet addresses, personal IDs, or “verification” documents | “Send a screenshot of your ID so we can verify you for the fund. It’s standard KYC.” |

### 1.2 Why Dating Apps Are the Perfect Hunting Ground  

- **Anonymity + low friction**: Users can create multiple avatars with minimal verification.  
- **Emotional vulnerability**: Loneliness amplifies the desire for connection, lowering skepticism.  
- **Cross‑platform migration**: Scammers move the conversation to WhatsApp, Telegram, or private crypto exchanges where platform moderation is weak.

## 2. The 5‑Step “Butcher‑Block” Protocol  

> **Goal:** Detect, isolate, and neutralize the scam before any money changes hands.  

### Step 1 – **Profile Forensics**  

- **Reverse‑image search** every profile picture.  
- Check the **username** on *HaveIBeenPwned* and *Google* for duplicate usage.  
- Look for **missing verification badges** on the app (e.g., FlirtCheck’s blue check).  

**Chat Example:**  

```
You: Hey, love your travel pics! 🌍
Scammer: Thanks! I’m actually on a photo‑shoot in Bali right now.
You: Cool! Mind sharing the link to your Instagram? I love to follow new creators.
```  

*If they refuse or give a generic link, flag immediately.*

### Step 2 – **Conversation Audit**  

- **Keyword radar:** “crypto”, “investment”, “high return”, “WhatsApp”, “wallet”, “Sha Zhu Pan”.  
- **Sentiment shift:** Sudden move from personal talk to financial talk within 5‑7 messages.  

**Chat Example:**  

```
You: How was your day?
Scammer: Busy! I just closed a $10k crypto trade that doubled in 48 hrs. Want in?
```  

*Red flag: financial pitch before any genuine rapport.*

### Step 3 – **Verification Challenge**  

- Request a **live video call** with a **specific background** (e.g., a book you mentioned).  
- Ask for **a photo holding a piece of paper** with today’s date and a random code.  

**Chat Example:**  

```
You: Let’s do a quick video call? Hold up a paper that says “FlirtCheck2026”.
Scammer: Sorry, my camera’s broken. I’ll send a selfie later.
```  

*Refusal = high probability of fraud.*

### Step 4 – **Secure Channel Freeze**  

- **Never** share personal IDs, banking info, or crypto wallet addresses.  
- If the scammer pushes to WhatsApp, **reply**: “I’m only comfortable staying on the app until we’ve verified each other.”  

### Step 5 – **Report & Recover**  

- Use the app’s **report** function (select “Financial Scam”).  
- Document screenshots (timestamps, URLs) and submit to **FlirtCheck Verified Portal** for community alerts.  
- If you’ve sent money, contact your bank **immediately** and file a police report referencing **FTC case #** (if applicable).

## 3. Real‑World Scenarios & Counter‑Scripts  

### 3.1 The “Sha Zhu Pan” Crypto Romance  

**Scammer’s line:** “I’m part of a Sha Zhu Pan group that’s making 300 % returns. I can add you to the private Discord.”  

**Your response:**  

> “I’m intrigued, but I need to see a **verified transaction receipt** and a **KYC document** that matches your ID. Also, can we keep the conversation on the app until we’ve both completed those steps?”  

*Result:* Most fraudsters will either panic or provide a **fabricated screenshot**—which you can later verify via blockchain explorers.  

### 3.2 The WhatsApp Investment Pitch  

**Scammer’s line:** “My friend runs a WhatsApp fund that’s only open to 10 people. Send $500 to lock your spot.”  

**Your response:**  

> “I appreciate the offer, but I only invest after **independent research** and **third‑party verification**. Could you share the fund’s **registration number** and a **link to a regulated broker**?”  

*Result:* Legitimate investors will comply; scammers will either **evade** or **press** with urgency.  

### 3.3 The Fake Crypto Exchange Romance  

**Scammer’s line:** “I’ve built a crypto exchange for couples to trade together. Let’s start with a $100 test trade.”  

**Your response:**  

> “Sounds fun! Before we trade, can you provide the **exchange’s URL**, **SSL certificate details**, and a **review from a reputable site**? I’ll also run a **whois lookup**.”  

*Result:* Most fake exchanges lack proper SSL or have mismatched WHOIS data, exposing the scam.
