### 3️⃣ Step‑by‑Step Protocol: Verifying a Profile Photo  

1. **Capture the image** – On mobile, tap‑hold the picture and select “Copy Image URL.” On desktop, right‑click → “Copy image address.”  
2. **Google Lens scan** – Open Lens, paste the URL, and hit “Search.” Note any “Visually similar images” that show up on other sites.  
3. **TinEye check** – Paste the same URL into TinEye. Look for “Exact matches” on stock photo sites (e.g., Shutterstock, Unsplash).  
4. **VisionScout deep dive** – Use the VisionScout web portal, upload the image, and click “AI Scan.” Review the confidence score (≥ 85% = likely authentic).  
5. **Cross‑reference** – If any tool returns a match to a commercial site or a different dating profile, **flag** the user.  

**Decision matrix:**  

| Result | Action |
|--------|--------|
| **No matches** (all tools) | ✅ Proceed, but stay vigilant |
| **One low‑confidence match** | ⚠️ Ask for an additional photo (different angle) |
| **Two+ matches or high‑confidence AI flag** | ❌ Block & report; consider contacting FlirtCheck Verified Portal for further analysis |

> **You:** “Hey Alex, I love your travel pics! I ran a quick reverse‑image check just to be safe—some of the backgrounds looked familiar. Could you share a photo of you in a different setting? 😊”

> **Catfish (typical response):** “Oh, I don’t have any other photos right now, sorry!”  

> **You (if flagged):** “No worries, I totally understand. I’m just trying to keep things secure for both of us. If you’re genuine, maybe we could video‑chat or meet at a public spot soon?”

**Why this works:**  
- **Polite framing** avoids accusation.  
- **Safety rationale** signals you’re serious about security.  
- **Escalation path** (video chat) quickly confirms authenticity.

If the person backs off or offers vague excuses, **cut the conversation** and report the profile.
