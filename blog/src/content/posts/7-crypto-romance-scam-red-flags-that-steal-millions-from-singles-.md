A swipe left on a profile that never existed feels a bit like a 404 on a love‑router. ## 1️⃣ The Sweet Talk – Trust‑Building Scripts  

### Red Flag #1 – Over‑Polished Romance Scripts  

Scammers often replay the same pre‑written payloads across dozens of sessions. Symptoms include:

- Identical phrasing appearing in unrelated chats (copy‑paste, not a genuine conversation).  
- Compliments that skip the usual TCP three‑way handshake of curiosity (“You’re the most amazing person I’ve ever met, even though we just matched”).

**Mitigation Routine:**  

1. Copy the suspect line and run a reverse‑search.  
2. Fire a query that requires a unique, stateful response (e.g., “What was the name of the café you mentioned on Tuesday?”).  

| You | Them |
|-----|------|
| *“I love hiking too! Which trail did you say you’d go to?”* | *“Oh, the one near my house… I’m not sure of the name, but it’s beautiful.”* |

A vague answer is a SYN‑drop – terminate the session.

## 2️⃣ Money Moves – Crypto Requests  

### Red Flag #2 – “Investment” Pitch With Urgency  

Typical payload: a high‑yield crypto fund that needs a tiny seed investment, wrapped in a 24‑hour deadline and an obscure wallet address.

**Mitigation Routine:**  

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1️⃣ | Halt the flow. Do not follow any link. |
| 2️⃣ | Query the wallet on a block explorer (Etherscan, BscScan). Look for a brand‑new address with zero prior traffic. |
| 3️⃣ | Demand a video call to verify the peer’s visual endpoint. |

> **Scammer:** “I’ve got a private DeFi pool yielding 18 % daily. Send 0.5 BTC now and I’ll double it tomorrow.”  
> **You:** “That sounds risky. Show me the contract address and a screenshot of the pool’s performance on a reputable explorer.”  

If the counterpart aborts the handshake or raises the error flag, drop the connection.

---

## 3️⃣ Platform Paranoia – Sudden Channel Switch  

### Red Flag #3 – Immediate Move to Private Messengers  

A legitimate peer will usually stay on the originating platform until a stable session is established. Scammers jump to WhatsApp, Telegram or Signal within hours, citing “privacy” or “better voice quality”.

**Mitigation Routine:**  

1. Log the timestamp and requested app.  
2. Request a screenshot of the profile on the new app showing the same username.  
3. If the request is denied, terminate the chat.  

| You | Them |
|-----|------|
| *“I’m comfortable staying here for now; can we keep chatting on the app?”* | *“Sure, but I’ll send you my WhatsApp for faster replies. Here’s my number: +1‑555‑123‑4567.”* |

An immediate channel migration is a classic port‑scan attempt – treat it as hostile.

---

## 4️⃣ Profile Forensics – Fake Identities  

### Red Flag #4 – Stock Photos & Reverse‑Image Hits  

Scammers recycle royalty‑free or stolen images.  

**Mitigation Routine:**  

- Run the profile picture through Google Reverse Image or TinEye.  
- Inspect any EXIF data for location mismatches (if present).  

> **You:** “Your photo looks great! I found it on a travel blog—are you the same person?”  
> **Scammer:** “Oh, that’s just a coincidence. I love that place too!”  

Evasive replies merit a report to the platform’s abuse team.

---

## 5️⃣ Emotional Manipulation – The “Emergency” Play  

### Red Flag #5 – Crying Over a “Medical” or “Legal” Crisis  

Typical payload: a family member in the hospital, a bail bond, a legal fee – all payable in crypto.

**Mitigation Routine:**  

1. Request official documentation (hospital invoice, police report, court filing).  
2. Suggest routing the funds through a reputable charity instead of a private wallet.  

> **Scammer:** “My brother was arrested overseas, I need 2 ETH to bail him out.”  
> **You:** “I’m sorry to hear that. Can you share the case number or a link to the official court filing?”  

No paperwork? Close the socket.

---

## 6️⃣ Technical Jargon Overload  

### Red Flag #6 – Excessive Crypto Buzzwords  

Scammers pepper conversations with terms like “yield farming”, “staking”, “LP tokens”, often misused.

**Mitigation Routine:**  

- Fact‑check any claim (e.g., “Staking 5 % on X platform is impossible; the highest APY is 2 %”).  
- Ask for a plain‑English explanation and a verifiable reference.  

> **Scammer:** “We’ll lock your funds in a liquidity pool and earn 25 % APR instantly.”  
> **You:** “Can you show the pool’s contract address and a screenshot of the APR on a reputable site?”  

If the peer can’t produce a valid reference, the connection is compromised.

---

## 7️⃣ “Verified” Badges That Are Fake  

### Red Flag #7 – Self‑Declared Verification  

Some fraudsters paste a fabricated green tick onto a screenshot and claim platform verification.

**Mitigation Routine:**  

- Hover over the badge (if the UI permits) to see whether it’s a genuine element.  
- Cross‑check the user on the platform’s official verification page.  

> **Scammer:** “Look, I’m verified! See the green tick?”  
> **You:** “I don’t see the tick on my screen. Can you send a link to your verified profile page?”  

A generic URL or a static image is a clear sign of a spoofed badge.
