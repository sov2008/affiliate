## 1️⃣ The Sweet Talk: How Scammers Build Trust  

### Red Flag #1 – Over‑Polished Romance Scripts  

Scammers deploy pre‑written love letters that sound *too* perfect. Look for:

- Repetitive phrasing across multiple chats.  
- Excessive compliments that skip genuine curiosity (“You’re the most amazing person I’ve ever met, even though we just matched”).  

**Action Protocol:**  
1. **Copy a suspicious line** and Google it.  
2. **Ask a unique, personal question** that requires a specific answer (e.g., “What’s the name of the café you mentioned on Tuesday?”).  

**Chat Example:**  

| You | Them |
|-----|------|
| *“I love hiking too! Which trail did you say you’d go to?”* | *“Oh, the one near my house… I’m not sure of the name, but it’s beautiful.”* |

If they dodge or give vague answers, flag the conversation.

## 2️⃣ Money Moves: Red Flags in Crypto Requests  

### Red Flag #2 – “Investment” Pitch With Urgency  

Scammers often claim a **“high‑yield crypto fund”** they need a small seed investment for. Common triggers:

- **Time pressure** (“You have 24 hrs or the opportunity disappears”).  
- **Requests for obscure wallets** (e.g., “Send to this Trust Wallet address, not an exchange”).  

**Action Protocol:**  

| Step | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| **1️⃣** | Pause. Do not click any link. |
| **2️⃣** | Verify the wallet address on a block explorer (Etherscan, BscScan). Look for **zero transaction history** or **recent creation**. |
| **3️⃣** | Ask for a **video call** to see the person’s face and surroundings. |

**Chat Example:**  

> **Scammer:** “I’ve got a private DeFi pool that’s yielding 18% daily. Send 0.5 BTC to this address now, and I’ll double it for you tomorrow.”  
> **You:** “That sounds risky. Can you share the contract address and a screenshot of the pool’s performance on a reputable explorer?”  

If they refuse or become angry, **cut the conversation**.

## 3️⃣ Platform Paranoia: Unusual Communication Channels  

### Red Flag #3 – Sudden Shift to Private Apps  

A legitimate dater will usually stay on the dating platform until trust is built. Scammers **jump to WhatsApp, Telegram, or Signal** within hours, citing “privacy” or “better voice quality.”  

**Action Protocol:**  

1. **Log the switch request** (timestamp, app name).  
2. **Ask for a reason** and a **photo of the app’s profile** showing the same username.  
3. **If they refuse**, end the chat.  

**Chat Example:**  

| You | Them |
|-----|------|
| *“I’m comfortable staying here for now; can we keep chatting on the app?”* | *“Sure, but I’ll send you my WhatsApp for faster replies. Here’s my number: +1‑555‑123‑4567.”* |

**Red Flag:** Immediate move to a private messenger = high risk.

## 4️⃣ Profile Forensics: Spotting Fake Identities  

### Red Flag #4 – Stock Photos & Reverse‑Image Results  

Scammers recycle royalty‑free or stolen images.  

**Action Protocol:**  

- Use **Google Reverse Image** or **TinEye** on the profile picture.  
- Check **EXIF data** (if available) for location mismatches.  

**Chat Example:**  

> **You:** “Your photo looks great! I found it on a travel blog—are you the same person?”  
> **Scammer:** “Oh, that’s just a coincidence. I love that place too!”  

If the answer is evasive, **report the profile**.

## 5️⃣ Emotional Manipulation: The “Emergency” Play  

### Red Flag #5 – Crying Over a “Medical” or “Legal” Crisis  

Scammers fabricate emergencies (e.g., “My mother is in the hospital, need crypto for surgery”).  

**Action Protocol:**  

1. **Ask for official documentation** (hospital bill, police report).  
2. **Suggest a reputable charity** instead of direct crypto transfer.  

**Chat Example:**  

> **Scammer:** “My brother was arrested overseas, I need 2 ETH to bail him out.”  
> **You:** “I’m sorry to hear that. Can you share the case number or a link to the official court filing?”  

If they can’t provide it, **cut ties**.  

---  

## 6️⃣ Technical Jargon Overload  

### Red Flag #6 – Excessive Crypto Buzzwords  

Scammers sprinkle terms like “yield farming,” “staking,” “LP tokens,” often incorrectly.  

**Action Protocol:**  

- **Fact‑check** any claim (e.g., “Staking 5% on X platform is impossible; the highest APY is 2%”).  
- **Ask for a simple explanation** in layman’s terms.  

**Chat Example:**  

> **Scammer:** “We’ll lock your funds in a liquidity pool and earn 25% APR instantly.”  
> **You:** “Can you show the pool’s contract address and a screenshot of the APR on a reputable site?”  

---  

## 7️⃣ “Verified” Badges That Are Fake  

### Red Flag #7 – Self‑Declared Verification  

Some scammers claim they’re “verified” on the platform, but the badge is a **photoshopped screenshot**.  

**Action Protocol:**  

- **Hover over the badge** (if possible) to see if it’s a real UI element.  
- **Cross‑check** on the platform’s official verification page.  

**Chat Example:**  

> **Scammer:** “Look, I’m verified! See the green tick?”  
> **You:** “I don’t see the tick on my screen. Can you send a link to your verified profile page?”  

If the link leads to a generic URL or a screenshot, **don’t trust it**.
