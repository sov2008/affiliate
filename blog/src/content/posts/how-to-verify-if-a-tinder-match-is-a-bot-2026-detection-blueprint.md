## 1️⃣ Spot the Red Flags: Human vs. Bot Behavior  

### 1.1 Timing & Frequency  

| Indicator | Human Typical | Bot Typical |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **First reply latency** | 5 – 30 min (varies) | < 5 sec or > 2 hrs (auto‑reply) |
| **Message volume** | 1‑2 messages per day | 5‑10 messages in minutes |
| **Night‑time activity** | Sporadic, sleep patterns | 24/7 constant pinging |

**Protocol:**  
1. Note the timestamp of the first reply.  
2. If it’s instantaneous **or** delayed by several hours without a reason, flag it.  

### 1.2 Language Consistency  

- **Bots** often use generic compliments (“You’re gorgeous!”) followed by a rapid shift to a request (“Can I have your Instagram?”).  
- **Humans** mix personal anecdotes, ask follow‑up questions, and show occasional typos or slang.  

**Dialogue Example:**  

| Bot‑like | Human‑like |
|----------|------------|
| *“Hey beautiful, you’re stunning! 😊 Want to chat on WhatsApp?”* | *“Hey Maya! I noticed you love hiking—what’s your favorite trail in the Rockies?”* |

### 1.3 Profile Content Gaps  

- **Missing bio** or a bio that’s a copy‑paste of generic phrases (“Just here for fun”).  
- **Too‑perfect photos** (studio lighting, no background).  

**Action:** Screenshot the profile and run a quick visual audit (see Section 2).

## 2️⃣ Technical Fingerprinting: The Digital Forensics Toolbox  

### 2.1 Reverse‑Image Search  

1. **Take a screenshot** of the profile picture.  
2. Open **Google Images** or **TinEye** on a desktop.  
3. Drag the image into the search bar.  

- **Result A:** Multiple matches across unrelated sites → **Likely a bot** (stock photo or stolen).  
- **Result B:** Only appears on the dating app → **Possibly genuine** (but still verify).  

### 2.2 EXIF & Metadata Scan  

If the image is a direct upload (not a stock photo), you can extract hidden metadata:  

- Use a free tool like **ExifTool** (run `exiftool image.jpg`).  
- Look for **camera model, GPS coordinates, or creation date**.  
- **No metadata** or generic “Adobe Photoshop” tags often indicate a fabricated image.  

### 2.3 URL & Link Vetting  

Bots love to drop “quick chat” links.  

- **Hover** (or long‑press on mobile) to view the full URL.  
- **Check** for:  
  - Misspelled domains (`instagrm.com`).  
  - URL shorteners (`bit.ly`, `tinyurl.com`).  
  - HTTPS vs. HTTP (secure sites use HTTPS).  

**Protocol:** Copy the link into **VirusTotal** before clicking.  

### 2.4 AI‑Generated Face Detection  

2026 tools like **DeepDetect.ai** can flag AI‑generated faces. Upload the profile picture; a confidence score > 80 % = probable bot.

## 3️⃣ Conversation Testing: The Human‑Only Probe  

Bots stumble when forced to answer **subjective, time‑sensitive, or multi‑step** questions. Use the “**Three‑Layer Probe**” technique:  

| Layer | Sample Prompt | Expected Bot Response |
|-------|---------------|-----------------------|
| **1 – Personal Detail** | “What’s the name of the coffee shop you mentioned in your bio?” | Generic “I love coffee!” or “Which one?” |
| **2 – Temporal Context** | “If we met tomorrow at 7 pm, would you prefer a rooftop bar or a park?” | Immediate “Sure!” without preference |
| **3 – Counter‑Question** | “What’s a song that always reminds you of your hometown?” | Bot may repeat a popular lyric or give a vague answer |

**Dialogue Example:**  

```
You: Hey Alex, you said you love live music. Which venue in Austin do you hit up most?
Bot: I love live music! 🎶
You: Cool! What’s the name of the bar you mentioned last week?
Bot: I’m not sure, maybe you can suggest one?
```

The bot’s inability to **reference prior details** is a dead‑giveaway.
